## 📌 Project Overview

This project investigated the performance of a **Horizontal Subsurface Flow Constructed Wetland (HSSF-CW)** for the treatment of domestic wastewater.

The study combined a literature review of HSSF constructed wetlands with experimental evaluation of a pilot-scale system. The treatment system consisted of layered sand and gravel media with **Typha** vegetation and operated under horizontal subsurface flow conditions.

The study focused on pollutant removal, hydraulic behaviour, water-quality changes and the potential reuse of treated wastewater for irrigation.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

- Evaluate the treatment performance of an HSSF constructed wetland.
- Study the removal of organic matter and suspended solids.
- Analyse changes in key water-quality parameters.
- Understand the influence of hydraulic retention time, flow rate and media characteristics.
- Review the development and application of HSSF-CW systems under different climatic conditions.
- Assess the potential of treated wastewater for agricultural irrigation reuse.

---

## ⚙️ Experimental Setup

The pilot-scale HSSF-CW system was operated at approximately:

- **Flow rate:** 3.6 mL/min
- **Hydraulic Retention Time (HRT):** ~3.5 days
- **System dimensions:** ~52 × 40 × 25 cm
- **Plant:** Typha
- **Media:** Sand and gravel in layered configuration
- **Flow:** Horizontal subsurface flow

The system configuration and media arrangement are illustrated in the report's schematic diagram.

## 📊 Key Observations

The experimental results showed substantial reductions in suspended solids and turbidity.

### Total Suspended Solids
TSS decreased from approximately **1000 mg/L to 80–90 mg/L within the first day**, indicating strong filtration and sedimentation within the wetland media.

### Turbidity
Turbidity decreased from approximately **14 NTU to below 2 NTU within the first day**.

### COD
COD showed an overall reduction from approximately **170 mg/L to around 90 mg/L**, although fluctuations occurred during the experiment.

### BOD
BOD decreased from approximately **150 mg/L initially to around 65 mg/L** towards the end of the study.

### Nitrogen Transformation
Nitrate and nitrite concentrations showed fluctuating behaviour, indicating ongoing nitrogen transformation processes within the wetland.

### pH
pH remained approximately within the **6.4–7.0** range during the study.

## 🔬 Literature Review

A systematic literature review was conducted using the **Scopus database**.

Studies published between **2015 and 2025** were considered, focusing on HSSF constructed wetlands treating domestic or municipal wastewater and reporting quantitative performance data.

The review compared:

- Wetland dimensions
- Hydraulic retention time
- Hydraulic loading rate
- Media characteristics
- Plant species
- Pollutant removal efficiencies
- Climatic conditions
- Potential for wastewater reuse

The literature review showed that HSSF constructed wetlands have been applied across tropical, subtropical, temperate and cold climatic conditions. 

---

## 🌱 Irrigation Reuse Potential

The study explored the possibility of using treated wastewater for agricultural irrigation.

Although improvements were observed in several water-quality parameters, further evaluation is required before direct irrigation application. Important parameters such as EC, TDS, nutrients and pathogens require additional assessment and comparison with appropriate irrigation-water standards.

Future work proposed in the study includes seed-germination experiments using treated wastewater and larger-scale field evaluation.
